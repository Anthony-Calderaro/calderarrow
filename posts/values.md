Values
Why do we have them?
Why are they so cringe?
What makes them good, not cringe?

Test for a value: Check if the negation is a good value
- if so, then both values are good
- if not, the value is not good

good value is different from a good value to have. "We value people who do not commit mass murder" is a good value to have, but not a good value to share.

Good values are unique, and tend to accept a tradeoff. We value A, which gives us Y at he cost of Z. If someone can say We value B, which gives us Z at the cost of Y, that is likely a good value because it provides insight into their decisionmaking and culture. 

A bad value is one which does not distinguish your group from others. For instance, saying  we value hard work is stupid, because no one wants to associate with people who don't value working hard. It doesn't help people self-select for or against your culture. 

In a way, a good value shows a weakness. Is a football team right to say they value Offense at the cost of defense? Perhaps that means they will pay their offensive players more, or invest more in offensive coaches. But if another team said they value Defense at the cost of offense, are they any more or less wrong? We can debate it, but it ultimately doesn't matter -- because it doesn't show an end result, but provides insight into how the organization is going to strategize to win.

Values are good because you diversity of thought and homogenity of values. Otherwise, your organization fights internally about _planning_ rather than _executing_. Once a team has aligned on a value, it wants diverse thoughts to brainstorm approaches which align with that value.
