In neoliberalism, the system is designed around one goal in mind: Security. Security comes from exerting power over others. Traditionally, this has been with a military (hence, the United State's military industrial complex). A country with the population of a few million can rival a country with larger land mass and population because of the exponential destructive power of nuclear weapons.

This meant that the system which worked for a millenia is vulnerable. What worked for the Romans, the Greeks, the Mongols, the Persians, the Russians, the Prussians, the French, the Brits, was no longer a strong system. We needed something more than just physical strength and numbers.

True security comes from power. So the question is: how can you influence people when you can't use force? In the past, we could conquere, enslave, subjegate. No more.

## The System

the market should be dynamic. So we want minimal influecne from governments and militaries. We need democracy, so people are empowered the make choices which we can influence.

This is why communism can't work. Or other types of market regulations like tarifs.

The trade-offs:

1. minimal government intervention means things are driven economically. This results in a race-to-the-bottom for any good or service. Whoever offers the same quality for cheaper will win.
   Pro: Costs decline for goods and services. People become wealthier even if they do not earn more money, because their dollar can purchase more.
   Con: Any industry lacking a market monopoly (economies of scale, high barriers to entry, etc) will be eventually go to the lowest-cost-of-labor

Translation: Labor will always need to work to change and move up the economic ladder. If they don't, they will lose their job. The only way to insulate yourself is to obtain capital

2. Without a market monopoly, true power is driven by consumers, not producers. If no one can afford your product, it doesn't matter how many you make or how cheap they are. You have no business.
   Con: Manufacturing goods which have no market monopoly will go to lower-labor countries.

The result of this is:
A country which has more capital than labor will be wealthier. (CApital is easier to move than labor)
The country which buys has power (demand drives value)
Labor is subjectated by capital.

This last one is most important, but manifests in different ways:

1. Labor must move up the knowledge ladder to make things that are more complex or have natural barriers to entry _on the labor side_. Economies of scale are a barrier to entry _for capital_. If they don't, they will be without a job.
2. In exchange, labor captures a higher portion of their value and sooner. An employee gets paid every 2 weeks and captures 100% of their earnings (less taxes, etc). The business takes a smaller margin over time.

- Capital is more profitable in the long-run. Labor is more profitable in the short term

3. If you do not do 1, you must move to the capital class. If you do not do either, you will be poor.

## How this system beneifts america

1. American Capital has economic power over others. China is dependent on us because if we stop buying, they lose economic power. Our capital can invest in their competitors and drain them of economic power.

- Note: American Labor does not have economic power.

2. American labor is forced to advance up the economic ladder and produce goods and services with higher market barriers to entry. During this time, markets are most profitable. Once the barriers drop, profitability declines.

- Note: This means investing in america is always best, since we force
  This positions America as the best to invest in: Our capital (companies) have capital barriers to entry (economies of scale) and leverage cheaper labor costs abroad. Our labor is on the cutting-edge in higher-margin labor industries.
- Brain drain is a natural consequence. Why would your best and brighest stay to work for less when they can make so much more coming to America?

Note: This system is dependent on the free movement of labor. If we tighten our borders, prevent the brains from draining to us, they will drain to a competitor.
Look at how our allies are positioned: Scandenavian countries have tight immigration laws. Talent does not drain to them as easily, and so they have comfortable lives, at the cost of true power. They are dependent on allies.
Look at how our enemies are positioned: Abusive, authoritarian countries do not provide the market incentives for people to pursue their passions and achieve potential. They rely on coersion and force instead of money.

To be a leader in this system, you need:

1. Minimal market intervention
2. Reasonably open borders to facilitate brain drain and capital flight.
3. WMDs and military stregnth (to break the legacy system built on military force)
4. Labor markets at the highest rungs of the ladder to incentives foreign investment
5. Capital markets at the highest rungs to incentivize foreign investment

If you have these things, you have power over every participant in your system.

But we're left with a question: Why would anyone participate?

## Economics is about Value

That's the lede. Economic output is measured in value. If I pay you $1, that means you provided me _at least_ $1 of value. That means that the economic value represents the floor of the true value.

Value generation is based on the fundamental idea that time is limited, and therefore, the amount of things we can do with our time is limited. If we can get more things, more done, our lives receive more value than otherwise.

So why would someone participate in this system? If doing so provides them with economic value.

## Why not others?

The most common alternative is a highly planned, command economy. While efficient, they are not adaptable, and rely on the threat of force and restriction on freedom. There are 2 vulnerabilities with this:

1. The reliance on force is inherently inefficient. Rather than using your people for economic output, you employ people to subjegate others. This is better than people loafing off, but not as efficient as _incentivizing them to do whta they should be doing_.
2. The restriction on freedom goes against human nature. We like heirarchy and structure, so long as we have choice (or the illusion of choice). All else being equal, a country which operates a more free society will draw people who value freedom. And freedom of thought is what drives innovation at the cutting edge of technology.

Capital competition over labor: can't have strong tax laws, can have stronger labor laws because labor is higher value. It is easier for countries to compete on tax law, since there's no moat. There is a tipping point where increasing rates would drive business away, and it becomes a lower point as morecountries compete.

Labor demands drop, so population demands drops. The leading company must be diverse, and not beholden to ethnic justifications (we need to protect our borders/preserve our ethnic heritage). Brain drain requires saying yes to a foreigner.
