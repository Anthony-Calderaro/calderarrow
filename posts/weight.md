Fitness: body is a machine. Understand the machine and it serves you well. All machines break over time. All machines are eventually obsolete. You are no different. Body needs energy to work. Like an engine, you have to provide gas. For the body, it is a calorie. What happens if you have a ten gallon tank and out fifteen gallons of gas in? It leaks out. That's actually quite wasteful, and your body is smarter: Your body doesn't have a way to "leak" energy, so it stores it as fat. It's important to understand that you need some stores in case you don't have access to food as soon as you're empty. Like filling up at a quarter tank — you could drive longer, but why risk it? Your body needs energy, and unused energy becomes fat. Energy comes from food containing calories.
Why don't we store as much energy as possible? Because after a certain point, fat becomes a hindrance. You want your engine oiled, but too much is bad. Just like too little is bad.
Too much energy, engine doesn't work well. Harder to move, harder to breath, harder to circulate blood. Too little, it sputters out. Can't move. Can't breathe, can't circulate blood.
Eat food that tastes good and allows you to do the things you enjoy. If you're too heavy to do something you love, lose weight. If you're too weak to do something, gain weight.
Likely you'll want to gain strength. For that, you need muscle. Muscle growth is complex, but I can get you 90% of the way there with a few generalizations and shortcuts:
Building muscle is like building a car. You need mechanics, materials, and motivation. If you lack any of the three, you won't build a car. Same with muscle.
For muscle, your mechanics are proteins. You need protein, and a good approximation is to take your body weight (in pounds) and eat that many grams of protein each day. 150 lbs means 150 grams each day. If you're in kilos, double it.
It's important to note that you need to eat this every day. Even on days you rest. If you're doing everything else but not building muscle, you're likely not eating enough protein. More than this isn't worthwhile though. So if you're sure you're getting enough protein, check one of the other two.
Your mechanics are stimulation from the muscles you need to tell them to build. For that, you need to work. Reps x sets x weight equals volume. 10 sets per week, 5 reps per set. Last rep should be a struggle. If it's too easy it won't work. It needs to be hard enough that your body says "I would rather work on building a stronger engine than work this hard in the future"
The motivation is energy. You need energy to build, so you'll likely need to eat more unless you have a good amount of fat stores. If you're at or below 20%, you probably need to eat more.
